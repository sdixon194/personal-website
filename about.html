About

Me

My name is Steve, and I'm a software engineer by trade. I'm self taught, having started my role in tech as a technical support engineer for WordPress.com at Automattic, then graduating through their developer apprenticeship program to become a full time developer. My background is fairly varied. I have a degree in cinema, and have done a lot of freelance video work (and won some cool awards). I worked at the film desk of the Museum of Modern Art, which is where I learned how to communicate and talk with customers. I've had a lot of odd jobs, including digital marketer, bus driver, lineman flagger, and volunteer firefighter. I've learned a lot from each industry and the varied people I've had the pleasure of working with over the years. I bring a little bit of everywhere I've been and everyone I've worked with wherever I go.

Outside of my professional career, I am always picking up some new hobby to tinker with. I used to help run a board game design club in NYC that hosted workshops where we would design and build a board game in two hours. I currently help run a local mahjong club to teach people how to play the game and its many variations. I spend time designing and building video games (starting small, but the scope is always growing). I dabble in electronics repair and collect old consoles to fix up when I'm not building SNES cartridges from scratch. I play guitar and write music occasionally. My wife and I have a cat who is so so so cute.

This Site

This site is built with HTML, CSS, and some vanilla JavaScript. No generative AI/LLMs were used or consulted when building it. It intentionally avoids dependencies and frameworks and isn't very slick. I believe it's imperative to be able to carve a place of your own on the internet with as little friction as possible between you and the people you're communicating with. Walled gardens and internet censorship by governments and corporations aren't going away, but the tools to create something for yourself - and that you own - are still available to us. The old internet is still here, no matter how much we're told otherwise.
